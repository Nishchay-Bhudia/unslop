# Human vs. AI Content: The Data

This is the evidence behind why this skill refuses to fabricate authority and
insists on real specificity instead of generic polish. These are measured
outcomes, not intuition — treat them as the "why" behind every rule in
`SKILL.md` and `platform-rules.md`.

## Audiences can tell, and it costs engagement

- Roughly half of consumers can correctly identify AI-generated copy when
  they see it.
- A majority notice AI-sounding content on their feeds "often or very often."
- Engagement measurably drops (roughly 12%) the moment an audience decides a
  piece of content is AI-generated — not because AI content is worse per
  word, but because detection itself changes how the content is read.
- Trust in a brand drops sharply once an audience suspects undisclosed AI
  use, and purchase consideration drops alongside it.
- Preference for AI-labeled creator content has been falling year over year,
  not rising — this is a closing window, not an emerging one.

**What this means for the skill:** the goal was never to make content
indistinguishable from AI by hiding the tells. It's to make the content
genuinely specific and voiced enough that there's nothing generic left to
detect. See the Guardrails in `SKILL.md` — the same guardrail against
fabricating claims is also what protects against writing something that
reads as hollow the moment someone looks twice.

## Human-written content converts better, not just "feels" better

Comparative traffic data on human-written vs. AI-generated content shows a
large, consistent gap: human-written content drives several times more
traffic, several times the click-through rate, and meaningfully longer
session duration, with a noticeably higher bounce rate on the AI-generated
side (people leave AI content faster once they land on it). Over a
multi-month window, unedited AI captions showed real conversion decline and
high bounce rates, while human-written captions sustained far more clicks
and traffic per post.

## The hybrid model outperforms either extreme

This is the most important finding for how to actually use this skill day to
day: a **70% AI-assisted / 20% human-written / 10% real-time** content mix
outperformed both pure-AI and pure-human approaches, by a wide margin over
pure human-only content and by a smaller but real margin over AI-only
content.

The lesson isn't "avoid AI." It's "use AI for the routine 70%, and protect
real, unedited human voice for the 20% that's supposed to carry vulnerability
or a real moment — and don't try to automate the reactive 10%." A content
calendar structured this way outperforms either "everything AI" or
"everything hand-written." See the **Content-mix planning** advanced option
in `SKILL.md`.

Platform-specific lift from AI-assisted (human-led, AI-supported) content
over human-only varies a lot — it's largest on TikTok and smallest on
YouTube/LinkedIn, which lines up with how differently paced those platforms
are: TikTok rewards volume and iteration speed, LinkedIn rewards a slower,
more considered voice.

## Real, non-fabricated case studies

These are included as reference points for *why* certain moves in this skill
work — not as templates to copy verbatim.

- **Duolingo (TikTok)** grew from a modest following to tens of millions with
  zero paid ad spend by prioritizing entertainment and a consistent character
  voice over direct selling — the brand almost never pitches the product
  directly in its top-performing content.
- **Scrub Daddy (TikTok)** built a large following on self-aware humor that
  openly acknowledges its own promotional nature instead of pretending to be
  organic — the audience is in on the joke, not fooled by it.
- **Khaby Lame (TikTok/Instagram)** built one of the platform's largest
  followings on almost no dialogue — the appeal is universal, wordless
  common-sense humor that doesn't rely on linguistic complexity or cultural
  specificity to land.
- **Glossier (Instagram)** consistently features real customers instead of
  polished models or studio photography — user-generated, unpolished content
  measurably outperforms branded studio content on this platform.

## What this means in practice

- Don't reach for AI-sounding polish when the moment actually calls for a
  real, specific, unpolished detail — that's precisely the content the data
  says outperforms.
- Don't hide that a post is AI-assisted by faking a fabricated personal
  story — that's the exact failure mode the trust-gap data describes, and
  it's the same thing the Guardrails section in `SKILL.md` already forbids.
- Treat the 70-20-10 split as a planning tool, not a purity test on any
  single post.
